WissKI DOI requests digital object identifiers for WissKI records, so they can be cited persistently.

---

A DOI is a promise: this identifier will resolve to this thing for the foreseeable future, regardless of where it is hosted. For research output that promise is what makes citation possible — a URL in a footnote rots, a DOI does not.

This submodule requests DOIs for WissKI entities, so individual records become citable objects rather than pages on a website that may move.

**Understand what a DOI commits you to before minting them.** Registering an identifier is an institutional undertaking to keep it resolving — through site migrations, platform changes and the end of the project that created it. Minting DOIs for records you may later delete or restructure is worse than not minting them, because a broken DOI damages trust in the whole registry. Decide what is DOI-worthy, what happens to an identifier when a record is merged or withdrawn, and who is responsible after the project ends.

The registration agency's own metadata requirements apply too, so the fields a DOI needs have to exist and be populated — which is a cataloguing requirement, not a technical one.

WissKI Getty AAT Adapter reads the Getty Art and Architecture Thesaurus, so objects and materials can be described in controlled terminology.

---

The AAT is the standard controlled vocabulary for describing art and material culture — object types, materials, techniques, styles and periods. Cataloguing "maiolica" or "lost-wax casting" against an AAT identifier rather than as free text is what makes a collection searchable alongside others and translatable without re-cataloguing, since AAT terms carry labels in several languages.

This adapter queries the AAT live through SALZ, so terms appear as data rather than as copied strings.

The reason it matters more than a local vocabulary is aggregation. A collection whose material terms are free text cannot be meaningfully filtered by material in a national or European portal; one catalogued against AAT can. That is usually the difference between a digitisation project that ends at the institution's own website and one that reaches an audience.

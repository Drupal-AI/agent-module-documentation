LMS Classes groups students into classes, so the same course can run for several cohorts with separate membership and progress.

---

A course is content; a class is a delivery of it. The distinction matters as soon as the same material runs twice — a spring and an autumn intake, two departments doing the same compliance training, a school running one syllabus across three year groups. Without classes, either the course is duplicated per cohort (and diverges) or everyone is in one undifferentiated pool (and reporting becomes useless).

This submodule adds that layer: students belong to a class, a class takes a course, and progress and reporting are per class rather than per site.

The practical payoff is in the questions it makes answerable. Which of my students have not finished module three? How did the autumn cohort compare with the spring one? Who is in this class at all? Each of those needs a cohort concept, and none of them is answerable from a flat enrolment list.

Because LMS is built on Group, classes fit the same membership and access model rather than introducing a second one — worth knowing when planning who may see whose progress.

- Separate a spring and autumn intake.

- Report progress per class.
- Compare cohorts on the same course.
- Find students who have not finished a module.
- List who is in a class.
- Assign a teacher to a class.
- Keep course content shared across cohorts.
- Avoid duplicating a course per intake.
- Enrol a group of students at once.
- Restrict progress visibility to a class.
- Archive a completed class.
- Plan compliance training by department.
- Reuse Group's membership model for cohorts.

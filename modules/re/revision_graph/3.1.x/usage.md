Revision Graph adds a tab showing an entity's revision history as a graph rather than a list.

---

Drupal's revision tab is a reverse-chronological list, which is adequate while history is linear. Once a site uses workspaces, content moderation with drafts alongside a published version, or translations that revise independently, history stops being a line — and a list cannot show that. Two revisions created the same afternoon may belong to different branches of the same page's history, and the list presents them as neighbours.

A graph shows the shape. Which revision was published, which was drafted from it, where a fork happened and where it was reconciled — those are structural facts a list flattens away.

Where this earns its place is an editorial dispute: someone's change disappeared, and the question is whether it was reverted, superseded by a draft made from an earlier revision, or lost in a translation workflow. The list makes that an archaeology exercise; the graph usually makes it obvious.

It adds a tab and nothing else — no permissions of its own, so access follows whatever governs the entity's revision tab. Worth confirming that on a site where revision history itself is sensitive, since a graph makes the history considerably easier to read.
